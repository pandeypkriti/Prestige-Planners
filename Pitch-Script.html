How to Pitch Benafsha — Turning the Audit into a "Yes"

Goal: make her FEEL why she needs a better landing page + website — and want YOU to build it. No ads. No jargon. No insulting her current site.

The golden rule

Never criticise her website. She's proud of it and she's new to this — attack it and she gets defensive and you lose her. Instead: praise it, then reveal the money she's leaving on the table. You're not the person pointing out flaws — you're the person who noticed her potential and already started fixing it.

The arc of the whole conversation is three beats:

1. Praise (lower her guard) → 2. Reveal the gap (make her feel the loss) → 3. Show your solution (the page you built).

Beat 1 — Praise first (2 minutes, genuine)

Open by showing you actually studied her business. This earns trust instantly.

"I spent time really looking at Prestige Planners — your site, your Instagram, your reviews. Honestly, you've done the hard part most new planners never do: you've got a real luxury brand, beautiful photos, five-star reviews already, and a genuine story people connect with. That's a strong foundation."

She relaxes. Now she's listening, not defending.

Beat 2 — Reveal the gaps as felt pain, not features

Here's the trick: don't say what's technically wrong — say what it's costing her. Translate every audit finding into a sentence about lost bookings, looking less premium, or brides not finding her. Even better: ask a question so she says the problem herself.

Use these. Pick the 3–4 that land hardest:

💸 "You look expensive — but you're hard to book."

  • "When a bride sees your site on her phone at midnight, what's the very next thing she does to book you?" → (she'll realise there's no obvious button; it's just a floating WhatsApp).
  • The pain: every second of hesitation is a lost lead. Top planners have a "Book a Consultation" button following you down the whole page. She doesn't.

🔍 "Brides are looking for you in places you're not."

  • "When someone in Dubai googles 'wedding planner near me,' or scrolls Pinterest and TikTok planning their day — do they find Prestige Planners?" → (no Pinterest, no TikTok, Google profile not optimised).
  • The pain: her ideal clients are actively searching — and landing on her competitors instead. She's invisible exactly where the buying happens.

🏆 "Your work is stunning — but your site undersells it."

  • Her portfolio is 30+ gorgeous photos with no story. The top planners (Couture, Vivaah) turn each event into a mini case study — "300-guest royal wedding at [venue], here's how we pulled it off."
  • The pain: "Right now your gallery looks like beautiful stock photos. Turn three of these into real stories and suddenly it's undeniable proof only YOU could have made."

🙈 "You have proof — and you're hiding most of it."

  • Her 7 five-star reviews live only on the homepage. Her founder story is buried on an About page with no photo.
  • The pain: "People buy luxury from a person they trust. Your story — aviation, hospitality, London to Dubai — is exactly what makes a bride choose you over a faceless agency. But it's hidden. Your face and your reviews should be the first things they feel."

🩹 The small tells that quietly cheapen the brand (mention gently, 1–2 only)

  • Her homepage tab literally reads "Event Agency" and her About page says "About Us — 1" (leftover template glitches).
  • The pain (say it kindly): "Little things like this are invisible to you but they whisper 'template' to a high-end client who's paying attention to every detail — which is exactly the client you want."

The emotional close of Beat 2:

"None of this means your brand is bad — it means it's underperforming. You've got a Ferrari brand running at half speed. Everything you need to win is already here; it's just not working together to actually book clients."

Beat 3 — The reveal: don't tell her, SHOW her

This is the whole strategy. Words don't close — the page does.

"So instead of just telling you all this… I went ahead and built it. Here — open this on your phone."

Hand her the landing page you made (live, on her brand, on her phone). Let her scroll. Watching her own business look like a high-end agency made it is the moment she sells herself.

Then land it:

"This is one page — a taster. Imagine your whole website feeling like this, showing up on Google and Pinterest, with your story and your work front and centre. That's what I'd love to build for you."

The two-tier offer (landing page now → website later)

Give her a small, easy yes first, then the bigger one.

Tier 1 — the landing page (the wedge):

"Let's start with this one page — it's your new 'front door' for anyone you send to Prestige Planners. Low commitment, fast, and you'll see the difference immediately."

Tier 2 — the full upgrade (the real project): Frame the audit's ⚠️ and 🔧 lists as the roadmap:

"Then, when you're ready, we build the full thing: a proper portfolio with real stories, your founder film, getting you found on Google, Pinterest and TikTok, and a site that quietly turns visitors into bookings. I've already mapped exactly what Prestige needs."

She sees you already have a plan → you look like the expert → she wants you to run it.

Three things to remember

  1. Praise → Reveal → Show. Never skip the praise.
  2. Sell the hole, not the drill — talk lost bookings and looking premium, never "SEO" or "CTAs."
  3. The built page closes the deal. Get it in her hands, on her phone, in her brand. Everything above is just the runway to that moment.
